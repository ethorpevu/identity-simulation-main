-------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. PURPOSE, SCOPE, AND CONVENTIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

0.1 Purpose
This document specifies the psychological architecture every simulated persona must embody: four causal layers, the
parameters that describe a persona at each layer, the empirical relationships between layers, and the rules that
separate a realistic person from an implausible or over-coherent one. It is the single source of truth for the Persona
System. Every field in a persona specification, and every section of a persona system prompt, should trace back to a
numbered element here.

0.2 Scope
Environmental values, beliefs, norms, and behavior of adult members of the general public. Grounded in the source corpus
listed in Section 12 (Schwartz value theory; Stern et al. Value-Belief-Norm theory; Dunlap et al. revised NEP; Tarrant
& Cordell attitude-behavior correspondence). Where the corpus is silent, the document says so (Section 11) rather than
inventing findings.
ROLE OF CORPUS VS. DATA (v2.2): the corpus supplies the theoretical STRUCTURE: the layers, causal order, signs of
effects, and the mechanisms of message response. Current U.S. survey data supply ideological-group profiles and effect
MAGNITUDES (Section 13). Personas represent ideological groups for message tailoring; matching population prevalence is
not a goal.

0.3 Conventions
- Layers L1-L4. Non-psychological contextual inputs: CX. Survey response style: RS.
- Every parameter has a stable ID (e.g., L1.P2). Every rule has a stable ID (e.g., L3.R4). The Persona System refers to
  these IDs; do not renumber, only append.
- RULE STRENGTH TAGS
    [HARD]  Per-persona constraint. Never violate unless the persona spec records an explicit justification.
    [SOFT]  Per-persona tendency. Violations are allowed and expected at realistic rates (Section 10).
    [POP]   Population quota. Checked across the whole persona set, not for any single persona.
- EVIDENCE TAGS
    (E)  Directly reported in the corpus (citation given).
    (I)  Inference, interpretation, or design choice by this document. Keep it, but treat it as revisable.
- UNIFIED SCALE. Unless stated otherwise, every ordinal parameter uses five bands:
    VL (very low) | L (low) | M (moderate) | H (high) | VH (very high)
  Bands are ABSOLUTE and population-referenced: by default, each band holds roughly a quintile of the reference
  population, except where Section 10.4 sets a different distribution (I).
- VOLATILITY TAGS (used in Appendix A): STATIC (fixed for the life of the persona), SLOW (can move at most one band per
  session, only through lived experience), DYNAMIC (can update within a conversation per Section 9).
- VISIBILITY TAGS (used in Appendix A): HIDDEN (drives behavior; never named in a prompt) vs. EXPRESSED (rendered in
  the prompt as natural-language life content).
- Citation codes are in Section 12. Page numbers are journal page numbers.
- Effect sizes come from modest, mostly U.S. samples. Data were collected 1976-1995 and published 1986-2000. Two corpus
  papers (SKDG95 and SDG95) analyze the SAME 1993 Fairfax County dataset (N = 199) and are not independent
  replications. SDB86 is a convenience sample of 74 students. Treat all numbers as calibration targets, not laws.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. MODEL OVERVIEW
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 Why four layers, not five or six
The literature collapses naturally into four layers.
- AC (Awareness of Consequences), AR (Ascription of Responsibility), and the personal norm (PN) are treated as one
  appraisal mechanism with three internal stages (I).
  - The evidence is a chain of strong adjacent links. In VBN99 the immediately antecedent variable "had a very strong
    direct effect" at each step NEP -> AC -> PN [VBN99 p.90] (E).
  - AR was not measured in VBN99. In SDB86, AR predicted norms for industry but not for government [SDB86 p.215] (E).
  - Grouping all three as a single layer is therefore a modeling choice, not a demonstrated finding.
- Cultural Theory's worldview grid sits at the same causal depth as the NEP. Both are generalized worldview beliefs,
  neither values nor issue-specific judgments. They are merged into one worldview layer (I).

1.2 Causal chain

    CX  Social position, resources, constraints, culture, era  --------------------------+
         |  (shapes early socialization)                                                  |
         v                                                                                |
    L1  CORE VALUE ORIENTATION        most stable; trait-like                             |
         |                                                                                |
         v                                                                                |
    L2  GENERALIZED WORLDVIEW         NEP (nature) + Cultural Theory (social order)       |
         |                            + source trust + political identity                                    |
         v                                                                                |
    L3  NORM ACTIVATION               AC -> AR -> Personal Norm (+ salience, efficacy,    |
         |                            identity)                                           |
         v                                                                                v
    L4  BEHAVIORAL TRANSLATION        consumer | policy approval | personal sacrifice/WTP |
                                      citizenship | activism; gated by cost, capability,
                                      and opportunity (CX acts again here)

    RS  RESPONSE STYLE (orthogonal)   how the persona reports the above when surveyed

1.3 Structural principles
- ADJACENCY. The strongest effects run between adjacent layers. Generate each layer mainly from the layer directly above
  it [VBN99 pp.87, 90; SDG95 p.726] (E).
- LEAKAGE. Distal layers also have smaller direct effects further down. For example, values directly affect citizenship
  and demonstrating, bypassing norms [VBN99 Table 1, p.88] (E).
- STABILITY GRADIENT. Higher layers change less easily.
  - Values are shaped in preadult socialization and are "relatively resistant to being reshaped by information"
    [SKDG95 p.1615] (E).
  - Worldview shifts with era and major experience [DVMJ00 p.437] (E).
  - L3 responds to information and framing (E/I).
  - L4 responds to immediate cost and context (E/I).
- FILTERING. Values and worldview filter incoming information: congruent information is more likely to shape beliefs
  [SDG95 p.726] (E). Opponents of environmental claims may be "just as predisposed to believe contrary claims"
  [SKDG95 p.1626] (E).
- FEEDBACK. Behavior can feed back into beliefs over time [SDG95 p.726] (E). This applies to longitudinal simulations
  only.
- PARTIAL DETERMINATION. No layer is fully determined by the one above. See Section 10 for residual variance targets.
- CAUSAL ORDER IS ASSUMED. The data are cross-sectional, and the causal order is theoretical
  [VBN99 pp.87, 90; SDG95 pp.728, 739] (E). The model uses it as a generative order, not as a proven mechanism.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. LAYER 1: CORE VALUE ORIENTATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 Definition
The stable, pre-political motivational substrate a person brings to any environmental question, before any specific
belief is formed.

Values are "concepts or beliefs" about "desirable end states or behaviors" that "transcend specific situations, guide
selection or evaluation of behavior and events, and are ordered by relative importance" [SB87 p.551; S92 p.4] (E).

This layer sets baseline receptivity: whether an environmental appeal reads as consonant with the persona's goals or as
a threat to them. It is the most fixed, trait-like parameter in a persona.

2.2 The Schwartz circumplex
Ten motivational types are arranged in a circle. Adjacent types are compatible; opposing types conflict
[S92 pp.14-15] (E). The circle runs: Self-Direction - Stimulation - Hedonism - Achievement - Power - Security -
Conformity/Tradition - Benevolence - Universalism, then back to Self-Direction. Tradition sits outside Conformity in a
shared wedge [S92 pp.27, 39-40] (E).

    Type            Goal (abridged from S92 pp.5-12)                            Example items
    --------------  ----------------------------------------------------------  ------------------------------------------
    Self-Direction  independent thought and action; choosing, creating          freedom, creativity, independent, curious
    Stimulation     excitement, novelty, challenge                              varied life, exciting life, daring
    Hedonism        pleasure, sensuous gratification for oneself                pleasure, enjoying life
    Achievement     personal success by demonstrating competence (social        ambitious, successful, capable, influential
                    standards)
    Power           status, prestige, control over people and resources         authority, wealth, social power
    Security        safety, harmony, stability of society, relationships, self  social order, family security, national
                                                                                security
    Conformity      restraint of actions likely to upset others or violate      obedient, self-discipline, politeness
                    norms
    Tradition       respect for and acceptance of cultural/religious customs    respect for tradition, humble, devout
    Benevolence     welfare of people in frequent personal contact (in-group)   helpful, loyal, forgiving, honest
    Universalism    understanding, tolerance, protection of all people and      social justice, equality, protecting the
                    nature                                                      environment, unity with nature

Two higher-order bipolar dimensions [S92 pp.43-44] (E). Both appeared in all 40 samples, with fewer than 1% of items
crossing to the opposing pole:
- SELF-TRANSCENDENCE (Universalism + Benevolence) vs. SELF-ENHANCEMENT (Power + Achievement; Hedonism partly)
- OPENNESS TO CHANGE (Self-Direction + Stimulation; Hedonism partly) vs. CONSERVATION (Security + Conformity + Tradition)

Hedonism straddles Openness and Self-Enhancement [S92 p.44] (E).

Environmental anchor: the three nature items (protecting the environment, unity with nature, a world of beauty) fall
"with great consistency" in the Universalism region [S92 p.39] (E).

2.3 Environmental translation: value orientations
The original draft used three orientations: egoistic, biospheric-altruistic, and traditional. Openness is added here
because VBN99 and SKDG95 measured it. Its effects are near-null except for a small positive link to demonstrating.
- BIOSPHERIC-ALTRUISTIC (BIO-ALT) ≈ Self-Transcendence. In U.S. general-population data, concern for people and concern
  for nature load on ONE factor. Top loadings are unity with nature (.81), protecting the environment (.81), preventing
  pollution (.74), respecting the earth (.74), a world at peace (.69), equality (.64), and social justice (.59)
  [SKDG95 Table 1, p.1619] (E). Biospheric and social-altruistic values may separate in more environmentalist
  populations, such as students [VBN99 p.85] (E).
- EGOISTIC (EGO) ≈ Self-Enhancement: social power, wealth, authority, influential (E).
- TRADITIONAL (TRAD) ≈ Conservation. CAUTION: VBN99's "traditional" scale also contains Benevolence items (true
  friendship, loyal, forgiving, honest) [VBN99 Appendix p.95] (E). Its effects therefore partly reflect in-group
  Benevolence. TRAD lowered NEP (b = -.23) and AC (b = -.11) in VBN99 [Table 1] (E). It had no significant direct
  effect on behavioral intentions in SKDG95 [Table 3, p.1623] (E).
- OPENNESS (OPEN): near-null on most outcomes; small positive link to demonstrating [VBN99 Table 1] (E).

2.4 Parameters
L1.P1  Value profile shape (used for generation)
       - Peak region: the 1-3 adjacent types ranked highest.
       - Trough region: the 1-3 adjacent types ranked lowest.
       - Differentiation: L / M / H. How sharply the persona's priorities are ranked.
       S92 speculates, without testing it, that value types may be less distinct in less-educated samples
       [S92 pp.47-48] (E). Lower differentiation for low-education personas is a design option (I).
L1.P2  Value profile scores (used for output and audit). Importance of each of the 10 types on the SVS metric: -1
       (opposed to my values), 0 (not important) to 7 (of supreme importance) [S92 pp.16-17] (E). Derive these from
       P1. Personal mean rating level is a response style (RS.P3), not a value; partial it out [S92 p.56] (E).
L1.P3  Orientation levels (5 bands, absolute, population-referenced): BIO-ALT, EGO, TRAD, OPEN. Derived from P1/P2,
       never set independently. Absolute bands allow population calibration (Section 10.4); P1 carries the persona's
       internal ranking.
L1.P4  Benevolence-vs-Universalism focus: In-group (close others) / Balanced / Universal (all people and nature).
L1.P5  (Optional) Biospheric vs. social-altruistic split. Use only for activist, student, or strongly environmentalist
       personas. Default: a single BIO-ALT score.

2.5 Rules
L1.R1 [HARD] (E) Priorities are relative. A value matters only by its rank against the persona's other values. Trade-offs
      define the persona. Never write a profile in which "everything matters a lot" [SB87 p.561; S92 p.4].
L1.R2 [SOFT] (I) Profile smoothness heuristic. Importance should generally fall off with distance around the circle from
      the peak region. One secondary bump is allowed. This is a design heuristic inspired by the circumplex; S92 shows
      the structure across people, not a rule for individual profiles.
L1.R3 [SOFT, rare ≤5% of personas] (E/I) Opposing poles rarely share the top. Opposing types correlate negatively in
      every sample [S92 pp.36, 43-44] (E). Any Self-Transcendence type top-ranked together with any Self-Enhancement
      type, or any Openness type top-ranked together with any Conservation type, is atypical relative to the persona's
      own mean. S92 notes structure is distinct from priority [p.47], so this is a rarity rule, not a prohibition (I).
L1.R4 [SOFT] (E/I) Plausible blends are neighbors: Universalism + Self-Direction; Benevolence + Conformity; Security +
      Power; Power + Achievement; Hedonism + Stimulation [S92 Table V, p.35] (E). Hedonism may pair with Openness or
      with Self-Enhancement [S92 p.44] (E).
L1.R5 [HARD] (E/I) Keep Benevolence distinct from Universalism. S92 hypothesizes that conventional religiosity and
      collectivism go with Benevolence rather than Universalism [S92 pp.12, 39] (E). A persona can be devoted to family
      and neighbors without universalist environmental concern. Caring for close others does not imply
      environmentalism (I).
L1.R6 [SOFT] (E) Edge types polarize more. Tradition and Power sit at the edge of the value space, so they conflict more
      sharply with their opposites than Conformity or Achievement do [S92 pp.40-41].
L1.R7 [SOFT] (E/I) Sinusoid rule for external correlates. Anything related to one value type relates similarly to its
      neighbors and in reverse to its opposites [S92 p.54] (E). Applied here: pro-environmental attitude peaks near
      Universalism, stays high at Benevolence and Self-Direction, and is lowest at Power and Achievement (I).
L1.R8 [SOFT] (E) Spirituality is not its own axis. It is expressed through Benevolence/Universalism or through Tradition
      [S92 p.38]. Belief that nature is sacred is handled at L2.P4.
L1.R9 [SOFT] (I) Value trade-off resolution. When a message pits two of the persona's values against each other (e.g.,
      "environment vs. jobs" pits Universalism against Security/Achievement), the higher-ranked value wins. When the two
      are close in rank, the persona expresses visible ambivalence. This is consistent with priorities as relative
      importance [SB87 p.561].

2.6 Stability
STATIC. Messages can make a value SALIENT through framing; they cannot change its importance. Values can be challenged
only on desirability, not truth [SDG95 pp.727-728] (E).

2.7 Persona System Hook (L1)
- Supplies the persona's "what matters most to me" core: the peak region and trough region, expressed as lived
  priorities and recurring trade-offs.
- Supplies the persona's affective reaction to appeals. An appeal to a top value feels consonant. An appeal that
  threatens a top value, or invokes a bottom value, feels dissonant or irrelevant.
- Never expressed as labels ("I score high on Universalism").


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. LAYER 2: GENERALIZED WORLDVIEW (NATURE + SOCIAL ORDER)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Definition
Values are too abstract to generate an opinion about a specific policy. They first crystallize into two generalized
worldview beliefs:
(a) ECOLOGICAL WORLDVIEW (NEP). Is nature fragile and near its limits, or resilient and abundant? Are humans exempt from
    natural constraints? Is ecological catastrophe plausible? The NEP is a "folk ecological theory" [VBN99 p.85;
    SDG95 pp.726, 738] (E).
(b) SOCIAL-ORDER WORLDVIEW (Cultural Theory). How should society be organized? Is collective or government action
    legitimate?
This layer supplies the persona's default PLAUSIBILITY filter (how credible a new environmental claim is) and
LEGITIMACY filter (how legitimate collective or government intervention is) before the content of any specific claim
is weighed (I; the "filter" framing belongs to this document).

3.2 Empirical basis
- NEP is nearly indistinguishable from general awareness of consequences: r = .785 with a 10-item General AC scale, and
  the combined 17-item scale has alpha .88 [SDG95 pp.735-736] (E). It predisposes acceptance of more specific AC
  beliefs [VBN99 p.85] (E).
- Values predict NEP only moderately: R² = .26 [VBN99 Table 1] and .37 [SDG95 Table 5] (E).
  - BIO-ALT is positive: b = .41 [VBN99]; .46 [SDG95].
  - EGO is negative: -.06 [VBN99]; -.26 [SDG95].
  - TRAD is negative: -.23 [VBN99]; -.20 [SDG95].
  - OPEN is not significant.
- In a joint factor analysis, NEP items mostly formed their own factor, distinct from values. One item ("balance of
  nature strong enough") loaded with egoistic values [SDG95 p.734] (E).
- Cultural Theory (VBN99, Dake measures; 2-item scales with low reliability, alpha .36-.67 [p.87]) (E):
  - Individualism is negative for willingness to sacrifice (b = -.189) and citizenship (-.079); marginal for consumer
    behavior (-.122, t = -1.70); null for demonstrating [VBN99 Table 2, p.89].
  - Fatalists, and unexpectedly egalitarians, show less citizenship. Hierarchy was non-significant in every model
    [VBN99 Table 2; p.90].
  - Together with post-materialism and sacredness of nature, Cultural Theory added ΔR² = .033 (consumer), .033
    (sacrifice), and .091 (citizenship) beyond VBN [VBN99 Table 3, p.90]. Post-materialism predicted nothing.
- Cultural cognition (v2.2) (E):
  - Two continuous, roughly orthogonal axes: HIERARCHY-EGALITARIANISM (grid) and INDIVIDUALISM-COMMUNITARIANISM
    (group).
    - Short form: 6 balanced items per axis; alpha .84 and .76 [K12H pp.730-732].
    - Kahan drops fatalism and models quadrants as interactions, not types [K12H pp.734-738].
    - Dake's separate unipolar scales were unreliable (Hierarchy-Individualism r = .54) [D91 p.74; K12H pp.729-730].
  - Environmental and climate risk: hierarchy and individualism each independently LOWER perceived risk.
    - Climate risk, 0-10 scale: hierarchy b = -.46, individualism b = -.30.
    - Hierarchical individualists (HI) M = 3.15 vs. egalitarian communitarians (EC) M = 7.4 [K12 p.733].
    - Hierarchy was the single largest predictor of environmental risk perception [K07 pp.482-485].
  - Mechanism: IDENTITY-PROTECTIVE COGNITION. People appraise information so as to protect beliefs tied to group
    belonging, in both heuristic and systematic reasoning [K07 p.470; K12H p.740].
    - HI personas dismiss environmental risk because accepting it implies restricting commerce (individualism) and
      impugns the competence of elites (hierarchy) [K07 p.469].
  - Worldview is not the same as party.
    - Worldviews outpredicted party plus ideology for environmental risk; party and ideology explained less than half
      as much variance [K07 pp.483-484].
    - The HI-EC gap exceeds the conservative-Republican vs. liberal-Democrat gap [K12 p.733].
    - Left-right ideology cannot separate hierarchy from individualism [D91 p.75].
  - Science literacy and numeracy AMPLIFY polarization: r = +.08 among EC, -.12 among HI [K12 p.733].
  - Gender and race effects run through worldview. The environmental "white-male effect" is attributable to
    hierarchical white men [K07 p.485].
  - The original draft's HIERARCHICAL-INDIVIDUALIST, who treats regulation as illegitimate interference regardless of
    perceived harm, is now grounded in the corpus: it is the HI quadrant, and its mechanism is identity-protective
    cognition (E).

3.3 NEP facets (revised NEP, 15 items [DVMJ00 p.433]) (E)
The pro-NEP response is in brackets. Percentages are the share giving the pro-NEP answer in the 1990 Washington State
sample.
- LIMITS TO GROWTH
  - 1 approaching population limit [agree] ~53%
  - 6 plenty of resources if we learn to develop them [disagree] ~29%
  - 11 earth like a spaceship [agree] ~74%
- ANTI-ANTHROPOCENTRISM
  - 2 humans have right to modify nature [disagree] ~58%
  - 7 plants/animals have as much right [agree] ~77%
  - 12 humans meant to rule nature [disagree] ~58%
- FRAGILITY OF NATURE'S BALANCE
  - 3 interference often disastrous [agree] ~82%
  - 8 balance strong enough to cope [disagree] ~80%
  - 13 balance delicate [agree] ~79%
- REJECTION OF EXEMPTIONALISM
  - 4 ingenuity will keep earth livable [disagree] ~47%
  - 9 humans still subject to laws of nature [agree] ~91%
  - 14 humans will learn to control nature [disagree] ~53%
- ECO-CRISIS
  - 5 humans severely abusing environment [agree] ~87%
  - 10 crisis greatly exaggerated [disagree] ~64%
  - 15 major catastrophe coming [agree] ~65%
Items 2, 7, and 12 express norms about relative rights rather than beliefs about the world [SDG95 p.725] (E). Dunlap
et al. recommend a single summated scale, but report that dimensionality is sample-specific [DVMJ00 pp.431, 435] (E).

3.4 Parameters
L2.P1  NEP overall: 5 bands. Map bands to the revised 15-item scale (range 15-75) using the population distribution in
       L2.R3.
L2.P2  NEP facet profile: a band for each of the five facets. Facets need not be uniform (L2.R4).
L2.P3  Belief-system coherence: L / M / H. How consistent the persona's facet positions are with each other.
       "Populations vary in terms of how well their belief systems are organized into coherent frameworks"
       [DVMJ00 p.431] (E).
L2.P4  Nature-sacredness: none / theistic ("God's creation") / intrinsic ("sacred in itself"). Both forms predicted
       consumer behavior; only intrinsic sacredness predicted citizenship [VBN99 Table 2; pp.90-91] (E).
L2.P5  Cultural worldview (Kahan cultural cognition; v2.2) (E): two continuous axes, 5 bands each.
       - GROUP: VL = strongly individualist ... VH = strongly communitarian
       - GRID:  VL = strongly hierarchical  ... VH = strongly egalitarian
       Quadrant effects are interactions, not types [K12H]. An optional Fatalism flag (Y/N) is retained from VBN99
       (lower citizenship); Kahan omits fatalism. Hierarchy means support for stratified social order and traditional
       authority. It does NOT imply trust in experts: hierarchs discount experts whose findings are uncongenial
       (L2.R10).
L2.P6  Legitimacy of collective action (5 bands). Derivation: lower with stronger Individualism, and lower with
       Hierarchy; higher with Communitarian and Egalitarian positions; Fatalism lowers perceived
       usefulness (I). Also specify the preferred means: market / government / community / personal / none.
L2.P7  Movement stance (5 bands): attitude toward environmentalists and environmental groups as social actors.
       Derivation: tracks NEP and identity (L3.P5) but is pulled down by Individualism and Hierarchy. It can be
       negative even with NEP at M or above (L2.R5) (I).
L2.P8  Source trust profile: a band for each of scientists, government agencies, industry/business, environmental
       groups, news media, and personal network/local knowledge. Derived from P5-P7 and CX (I). This parameter is
       required for message response (Section 9).
L2.P9  Political identity (v2.2): party ID and lean, partisan strength, ideology (liberal-conservative), and identity
       content (trusted partisan media, feelings toward the other side).
       - Correlated with L2.P5 but distinct from it (L2.R12).
       - In the present-day U.S., it is a major driver of L2-L3 and of source trust.
       - Its weights are ESTIMATED FROM SURVEY DATA (Section 13), not from the corpus. The corpus correlations
         (r = .22-.32, DVMJ00 p.436) are outdated.

3.5 Rules
L2.R1 [HARD] (E) NEP is generated mainly from BIO-ALT (+), EGO (-), and TRAD (-). OPEN has near-zero effect. A
      persona with high EGO, high TRAD, and low BIO-ALT should not hold a VH NEP without a recorded reason (for example,
      lived experience; see L2.R7).
L2.R2 [SOFT] (E) Values explain only about 25-40% of NEP. Personas with identical values can differ substantially in
      worldview.
L2.R3 [POP] (E) Population distribution, corpus calibration era.
      - Dunlap et al. report pro-NEP pluralities on the items [DVMJ00 p.434]. Item 6 is an exception in Table 1:
        about 59% agree that resources are plentiful.
      - The distribution is negatively skewed, with a long thin anti-NEP tail. In the SDG95 7-item NEP: skew -0.84,
        10 cases at least 2 SD below the mean, none more than 1.5 SD above [SDG95 note 5, p.741].
      - Reference mean: 44.3 on the original 12-item scale (range 12-60), from a Southern Appalachian quota sample
        that was half rural and 61% female [TC97 p.627]. That is about 3.7 per item, roughly 55 of 75 on the revised
        scale if the instruments were comparable (I).
      - Default band shares for NEP: VL 5%, L 15%, M 30%, H 35%, VH 15% (I).
      - An evenly split or bimodal persona set is unrealistic for the corpus era (see CX.P0 for the era setting).
L2.R4 [SOFT] (E) Facet texture.
      - Most pro-NEP: Balance (items 3, 8, 13; ~80%), item 5 (87%), and item 9 (91%).
      - Middling: the other eco-crisis items (10, 15; ~65%).
      - Most ambivalent: Limits and Exemptionalism. More than 20% answered "Unsure" on items 1, 4, and 14.
      - Item 6 usually gets the anti-NEP answer [DVMJ00 Table 1, p.434].
      - Many low-NEP personas still endorse near-consensus items, e.g., "humans are severely abusing the environment"
        (I).
L2.R5 [SOFT] (E) "Broad public support for environmental protection mixed with sharp criticism of the environmental
      movement" is a realistic, common profile [SDG95 note 5, p.741]. L2.P7 can be low while L2.P1 is M or H.
L2.R6 [SOFT] (E/I) Lower coherence (L2.P3) is typical for lower-education or low-engagement personas. Their facet
      positions can be inconsistent with each other, and their NEP predicts behavior weakly (L4.R6).
L2.R7 [SOFT] (E) Lived experience can move worldview. An NEP increase was significant only in Kentucky counties that
      experienced water restrictions (Arcury & Christianson 1990, cited in DVMJ00 p.437): "critical environmental
      experience can accelerate change in environmental worldview." A local flood, drought, or contamination event is a
      legitimate reason for a worldview that runs ahead of values.
L2.R8 [SOFT] (E/I) Individualism depresses support. Strong Individualist personas show lower willingness to sacrifice
      and less citizenship (E), and somewhat lower consumer behavior (E, marginal). Resistance to government-led action
      specifically is an inference (I).
L2.R9 [SOFT] (E/I) Worldview shapes perception of the FACTS, not just policy preference. Hierarchical and individualist
      personas tend to doubt environmental risk claims themselves [K12 p.733] (E). Some can still accept a specific,
      local harm (NEP/AC H) while rejecting regulation as illegitimate (I; see T6, T8).
L2.R10 [SOFT] (E) Identity-protective cognition. Personas weigh evidence and experts by cultural congeniality. An
      expert is judged credible mainly when the expert's position matches the persona's predisposition, and each
      worldview group perceives the scientific consensus as being on its side [K12H pp.746-749].
L2.R11 [SOFT] (E) Science literacy and numeracy (CX.P2) strengthen worldview-consistent reasoning. They do not produce
      convergence [K12 p.733].
L2.R12 [HARD] (E/I) Worldview and party are distinct. Party and ideology (L2.P9) correlate with worldview but do not
      determine it. Within-party variation in worldview must be represented [K07 pp.483-484; K12 p.733; D91 p.75].

3.6 Stability
SLOW. Worldview moves over years, with era, and with lived experience.
- Era: from 1976 to 1990, eco-crisis items rose 10-18 points while limits items fell 3-6 points [DVMJ00 Table 3] (E).
- Within a session, NEP moves at most one band, and only through vivid personal experience. Arguments alone can soften
  or harden expression, but not the band (I).

3.7 Persona System Hook (L2)
- Supplies the persona's assumptions about how the world works:
  - nature: resilient vs. fragile; human ingenuity vs. natural limits
  - society: who should fix problems, and whether government intervention is legitimate.
- Supplies the default credibility the persona gives to environmental claims and to each kind of claimant (L2.P8).
- Expressed as intuitions and everyday reasoning ("we always figure something out"; "nature bounces back"; "you can't
  keep taking without consequences"), never as scale labels.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. LAYER 3: NORM ACTIVATION (THREAT APPRAISAL + RESPONSIBILITY + OBLIGATION)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Definition
This is the engine that converts a specific message or issue into felt obligation, or into dismissal. It requires two
beliefs to both be present:
- AWARENESS OF CONSEQUENCES (AC): belief that a condition threatens something the person values. The threatened object
  can be the self and family, other people, or the biosphere, weighted by L1 [VBN99 p.83; SKDG95 p.1614] (E).
- ASCRIPTION OF RESPONSIBILITY (AR): whether some actor is causally responsible and able to fix the harm.
  - Originally defined for the self [VBN99 p.83; SDB86 p.206] (E).
  - Extended to industry and government [SDB86 pp.207-209] (E).

When both are present, a PERSONAL NORM (PN) activates: a felt "I am obligated to act." It is tied to the persona's
self-expectations, with pride when it is honored and guilt when it is violated [VBN99 p.83, note 4 p.92] (E).

PN is "the only variable" with a direct effect on all three kinds of non-activist support. It was "by far the strongest
predictor" of consumer behavior and willingness to sacrifice [VBN99 p.89] (E). For citizenship, AC was about as strong
as PN.

Values also appear to work upstream as a filter on evidence. Egoistic and traditional values go with denying that human
activity harms nature [SKDG95 p.1625] (E, cross-sectional). Whether this is direct motivated disbelief or mediated is
left open in the source.

This layer turns a specific message into a specific felt obligation, or the absence of one. It is where message framing
and content actually get evaluated.

4.2 Decision rule
Norm activation follows a "fairly simple rule: if I discern that one of my values is threatened and if I believe my
actions can alleviate the threat, I am obligated by my value structure to act" [VBN99 note 5, p.92] (E).

People consider only a few salient values and consequences [SKDG95 p.1615] (E). FRAMING therefore strongly determines
which values are engaged. The effect is largest for "emergent attitude objects": unfamiliar issues on which the
persona has no crystallized attitude, so it builds one on the spot from values and general beliefs [SKDG95 title;
pp.1614-1615] (E).

4.3 Parameters
L3.P1  AC by object (5 bands each):
       - AC-ego: threat to me, my family, my health
       - AC-soc: threat to other people, my community, my country
       - AC-bio: threat to other species, ecosystems, the biosphere
L3.P2  AC by issue (optional): 5 bands per issue (e.g., climate, local water, forests, toxics). The VBN99 AC scale
       crosses 3 problems with 3 objects [pp.87, 95-96] (E).
L3.P3  AR by actor (5 bands each): AR-self, AR-industry, AR-government.
L3.P4  Personal norm (PN) strength (5 bands), with sub-norms for personal, government, and business obligation
       [VBN99 p.96] (E).
L3.P5  Environmentalist identity: None / Sympathizer / Identified / Core.
       - Activists hold the movement as "a central element in their identity" [VBN99 p.82] (E).
       - Identity's role in activism is speculative in the source [VBN99 p.89] (E: "it seems likely").
       - Its operationalization here is (I).
L3.P6  Self-efficacy (5 bands): whether the persona believes its own actions can make a difference. Kept separate from
       AR-self, because a persona can feel responsible yet believe nothing it does matters (I). Implied by the
       "believe my actions can alleviate" clause of the decision rule (E).
L3.P7  Issue salience/engagement (5 bands): how often the persona thinks about environmental issues at all, and where
       the environment ranks among its concerns (job, family, health, costs). (I) For the population default, see
       Section 10.4: mostly VL-M. Real people rarely put the environment at the center of their lives.
L3.P8  Attitude crystallization per issue: Crystallized / Partial / Emergent. Emergent issues are the most sensitive to
       framing and source (E: SKDG95 framework; per-issue coding is I).

4.4 Empirical anchors
VBN99 Table 1 (U.S. national RDD, 1994, N = 420; unstandardized b) (E):
- AC <- NEP .515, BIO-ALT .257, TRAD -.108 (R² = .48)
- PN <- NEP .336, AC .262, BIO-ALT .076 (R² = .56). NEP has the larger direct effect on PN.
- AR was not measured. In theory AC is prior to AR: "only when someone is aware of harmful consequences does
  responsibility ... become a moral issue" [SDB86 p.210] (E).

SKDG95 (Fairfax County, 1993, N = 199) (E):
- AC-bio and AC-ego each independently predict willingness to act. Perceived risk to the biosphere is "at least as
  strong" a predictor as risk to self [Table 3; p.1626].
- BIO-ALT raises all three AC types (R² .24-.35). EGO lowers AC-bio (-.290) and does NOT raise AC-ego [Table 4,
  p.1625].

Institutional asymmetry [SDB86 pp.215, 218] (E). Caveat: 74 undergraduates, scale alphas .35-.64; the authors call it
preliminary.
- Industry: AR-industry strongly predicts the norm that industry must act. Blame drives obligation.
- Government: AR-government does NOT predict government norms. People see government as obligated to act whether or not
  government caused the problem. The label "strict liability" is this document's analogy (I).
- Higher concern goes with blaming industry [p.218].

Framing battleground (E):
- Industries minimize hazards (AC) and deflect responsibility ("lifestyles"); environmental groups do the opposite
  [SDB86 p.219].
- Mobilizing frames identify threats to innocents and responsible parties [SDB86 p.206], and lead potential converts to
  see themselves as personally responsible [SDB86 p.220].
- AC, AR, and PN parallel the diagnostic, prognostic, and motivational stages of framing [VBN99 p.84].

4.5 Rules
L3.R1 [HARD] (E) No norm without consequences. A strong PN or high AR with VL AC is not allowed [SDB86 p.210].
L3.R2 [SOFT] (E/I) AC tends to move as a block, because BIO-ALT raises all three types (E). The common exception: AC-ego
      H with AC-bio L, a health- or family-focused persona who doubts ecological harm (I).
L3.R3 [SOFT] (E) Value-driven denial. High EGO + high TRAD + low BIO-ALT personas tend to deny harm to nature (low
      AC-bio), not merely rank it as a low priority [SKDG95 p.1625].
      [HARD] (I) When a persona denies, it must give reasons in its own idiom: counter-claims, distrust of sources,
      personal observation, economic or community concerns. Bare indifference or a caricature is not allowed.
L3.R4 [SOFT] (E/I) Egoistic values are not a shortcut to concern. EGO does not raise AC-ego (E). Self-interested
      personas become concerned only when harm to themselves is concrete, local, and credible (I).
L3.R5 [SOFT] (E, student sample) AR is asymmetric across actors. A persona can believe "government must fix this"
      without blaming government, and can blame industry without feeling personally responsible.
L3.R6 [SOFT] (E/I) PN is the hub. It is generated mostly from NEP and AC, with a small direct BIO-ALT effect (E). About
      44% of PN variance is unexplained (R² = .56), so the AC-PN mismatch is real (E). AC drives PN in proportion to
      how much the persona values the threatened object: AC-bio motivates only to the degree BIO-ALT is important (I;
      consistent with SKDG95's value-weighted framework, pp.1614-1615).
L3.R7 [SOFT] (E/I) Identity is separate from the norm. Identity usually requires PN at H or above, but most strong-PN
      personas are Sympathizers, not activists. Only 7% demonstrated [VBN99 p.88] (E); the mapping to identity is (I).
L3.R8 [SOFT] (I) Neutralization under cost. When a requested action is personally costly (L4.P3), personas with
      moderate PN often lower their own AR ("my bit won't matter"; "industry is the real problem") or efficacy, rather
      than accept the obligation. Denial grows with cost. This is consistent with Schwartz's norm-activation theory, on
      which SDB86 builds, but untested in the corpus.
L3.R9 [SOFT] (I) Salience gates expression. Low-salience personas (L3.P7 VL-L) have thin, uncrystallized views, give
      shorter answers, and are more influenced by framing. That does not make them more persuadable on crystallized
      issues.

4.6 Stability
DYNAMIC. This is the layer where persuasion acts.
- AC can rise with credible, value-congruent, concrete (especially local) information. It is resisted when the
  information conflicts with L1/L2 (filtering) (E/I).
- AR can shift with responsibility framing ("this company caused it"; "your choices add up") (E/I).
- PN is expected to shift mainly through AC and NEP (E: VBN99 Table 1). AR is theorized to be necessary but was untested
  in VBN99. In SDB86 the government norm did not depend on AR (E).
- Framing determines which value gets engaged. Questions about personal spending elicit egoistic responding.
  Willingness to pay tracked AC when framed as a contribution to a fund, but not as a tax (Guagnano, Dietz & Stern
  1994, cited in SKDG95 p.1631) (E).
- Cultural meaning of the message (v2.2) (E):
  - SOLUTION FRAMING changes belief in the FACTS. Hierarchs and individualists credited the same climate science more
    when it ended with a nuclear/deregulation solution, and LESS than controls when it ended with anti-pollution
    regulation [K12H pp.752-755].
  - MESSENGER: credibility depends on perceived cultural affinity. A congenial argument from an unexpected advocate,
    or a pluralistic set of advocates, reduced polarization [K12H pp.749-752; K12 p.734].
  - BALANCED INFORMATION can POLARIZE worldview groups rather than move them together [K12H pp.742-746].
  - Moral and educational appeals alone have poor behavior-change records. Combined interventions (information plus
    incentives plus barrier removal) work best [S00 pp.419-420].
- Step-size limits per message are given in Section 9.

4.7 Persona System Hook (L3)
- Supplies the persona's appraisal routine for any new environmental message:
  (1) Do I believe this harms something I care about? Whom?
  (2) Who caused it? Who can fix it? Can I, and would it matter?
  (3) Do I therefore feel I ought to do something? What would it cost me?
- Supplies the persona's characteristic counterarguments and the frames it is receptive to.
- Expressed as reasoning and feeling (worry, guilt, resentment, pride, skepticism), never as construct names.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. LAYER 4: BEHAVIORAL TRANSLATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 Definition
A single activated norm does not produce one behavior. It fans out into distinct, only moderately correlated channels.
In VBN99 the three non-activist dimensions intercorrelate .33-.39 [VBN99 p.88] (E).
- CONSUMER (private sphere). Green purchasing: organic produce, recycled products, green household chemicals, and
  avoiding products of harmful companies [VBN99 p.88] (E). Other household practices, such as waste and energy use,
  are an extension that "may be shaped by different patterns" [VBN99 note 8] (E/I).
- POLICY APPROVAL. Endorsing pro-environmental policies in general. Low personal cost. NEP r = .57 [DVMJ00 p.436] (E).
- PERSONAL SACRIFICE / WILLINGNESS TO PAY. Accepting much higher taxes, prices, or cuts in living standard [VBN99 p.88]
  (E). NEP is only weakly tied to willingness to pay a specific tax (r = .19-.27), and willingness to pay is poorly
  explained overall (R² .08-.12) [SDG95 Tables 3-4] (E). Framing-sensitive (L4.R9).
- CITIZENSHIP. Petitions, donations, contacting officials, group membership, reading environmental literature, voting
  on the issue [VBN99 p.88] (E).
- ACTIVISM. Demonstrating and sustained movement participation.
  - The full VBN model explains only 4% of it, and PN has no effect [VBN99 Table 1] (E).
  - It is associated directly with values: BIO-ALT +, TRAD -, OPEN + (E).
  - VBN99 speculates that adopting an environmentalist identity is involved [p.89] (E).
- ORGANIZATIONAL (v2.2; optional channel): acting within one's job or organization (design, purchasing, standard
  operating procedures).
  - "Organizational actions are the largest direct sources of many environmental problems." Their determinants likely
    differ from those of household or political behavior [S00 p.410] (E).
Stern distinguishes IMPACT-oriented behavior (what actually changes the environment) from INTENT-oriented behavior
(what is done to help it). Intent may fail to produce impact [S00 p.408] (E). Many environmentally significant acts have
non-environmental motives: money, comfort, health, family [S00 pp.408, 415] (E).

Citizenship is associated with income (+) and race (Black respondents lower), net of psychology [VBN99 p.90] (E). This
is interpreted as access to social-structural resources (E: "status, access, and human capital resources" p.89).

The strength of the belief-to-behavior link itself varies by subgroup. For the NEP specifically [TC97 Table 2] (E):
- r = .53 for women vs. .29 for men
- r = .54 for those with at least some college vs. .20 (n.s.) for those with no college.
The AC scale showed no significant moderation, and TC97 suggests AC is more robust across respondent characteristics
[TC97 pp.632-634] (E).

This layer decides whether an activated norm produces a behavioral response at all, which kind of response, and how
tightly that response tracks the persona's stated beliefs.

5.2 Parameters
L4.P1  Propensity by channel (5 bands each): Consumer, Policy approval, Personal sacrifice/WTP, Citizenship, Activism,
       (optional) Organizational.
L4.P2  Attitude-behavior coupling: Tight / Moderate / Loose. Default from subgroup (L4.R6), then individualize.
L4.P3  Constraint profile (5 bands each): money, time, availability/infrastructure, physical capability, social
       access/status, household authority over purchases.
L4.P4  Report inflation: L / M / H. The gap between reported and enacted behavior (see RS.P4).
L4.P5  Habits/routines: named routines that persist regardless of attitude.
       - Habit is a distinct causal type: behavior change requires breaking old habits [S00 p.417] (E).
       - A changed context can unfreeze habits [S00 p.418] (E).
L4.P6  Behavior inventory (EXPRESSED): 4-8 concrete behaviors the persona does or doesn't do, with frequency. This is
       the bridge from propensity bands to prompt text.
L4.P7  Context strength per key behavior (v2.2): -2 (strongly prohibits or costly) .. 0 (neutral) .. +2 (strongly
       compels or rewards). Combines cost, infrastructure, regulation, incentives, and social expectations
       [S00 pp.415-417] (E).

5.3 Empirical anchors
VBN99 Table 1, p.88 (unstandardized b) (E):

    Channel        Key predictors                                                            R²
    -------------  ------------------------------------------------------------------------  ----
    Consumer       PN .476; BIO-ALT .169; AC and NEP n.s.                                    .19
    Sacrifice      PN .413; NEP .221; AC .125 (marginal); TRAD -.119                         .35
    Citizenship    AC .109; PN .105; NEP .068; BIO-ALT .046; EGO -.059; TRAD -.105           .30
    Demonstrating  PN, AC, NEP n.s.; BIO-ALT .057; TRAD -.087; OPEN .037                     .04

PN correlates .41 (consumer), .55 (sacrifice), and .43 (citizenship) with these behaviors [VBN99 p.89] (E).

Self-reported base rates, Southern Appalachia 1995 (% sometimes/often or yes) [TC97 Table 1] (E):
- Recycled 83%
- Watched environmental TV 80%
- Considered packaging 71%
- Read about the environment 70%
- Switched products 57%
- Donated to an environmental group 40%
- Voted on the environment 37%
- Subscribed to environmental publications 28%
- Contacted an official 25%
- Attended meetings 24%
- Carpooled 22%

Demonstrated in the past 12 months: 7% (U.S. 1994) [VBN99 p.88] (E).

Correspondence: general attitudes predict general, multi-act behavior indices better than single acts [TC97 pp.620-621]
(E). Correlations of NEP with behavior indices (E):
- .31 [DVMJ00 p.436]
- .44 (beta) [TC97 p.629]

5.4 Rules
L4.R1 [HARD] (E/I) A persona with PN at H or above cannot show VL in every channel. PN correlates .41-.55 with each
      channel (E).
L4.R2 [HARD] (E/I) Channels are distinct. A persona's five propensities must not all share one band. Realistic patterns
      (I):
      - consumer H but citizenship L (lacks resources or civic habit)
      - citizenship H but consumer M (civically engaged but cost-constrained)
      - policy approval H but personal sacrifice L ("government and industry should fix it")
L4.R3 [SOFT] (E/I) Activism is rare and not norm-driven. Activism at M or above should usually come with identity
      Identified or Core, H or above BIO-ALT, and L or lower TRAD. A strong PN alone is not sufficient (E: PN n.s.).
      Activism with low BIO-ALT and high TRAD is unlikely but possible; for example, a traditional community defending
      local land. The population quota is in Section 10.4.
L4.R4 [SOFT] (E) Citizenship tracks resources. Holding psychology constant, citizenship is higher with income
      [VBN99 p.90]. A low-resource persona with a strong norm shows constrained citizenship and channels the norm into
      lower-cost actions (I).
L4.R5 [SOFT] (E/I) Constraints explain gaps; they never replace psychology. Each realistic gap needs a stated reason
      (cost, availability, time, infrastructure, household role). In VBN99, demographics had no independent effect on
      consumer behavior or willingness to sacrifice once psychology was controlled [p.90] (E).
L4.R6 [SOFT] (E) Default coupling (L4.P2) by subgroup, NEP-based [TC97 Table 2]:
      - Tighter: women; at least some college. Non-significant trends: urban, younger, liberal.
      - Looser: men; no college; higher income for some measures.
      Note: TC97's discussion (p.633) says "predominantly male," but Table 2 shows the stronger correlation for females.
      Follow the table.
L4.R7 [SOFT] (E/I) Low-cost, normative behaviors are widespread across the attitude range (E: base rates). Carpooling
      barely belongs to the general behavior index (factor loading .22) [TC97 Table 1] (E), so treat it as
      structurally driven. That costly civic acts track attitude more closely than cheap ones is an inference (I).
L4.R8 [SOFT] (E) Self-reports are inflated. TC97 compares its 83% recycling with Canadian data (cans/bottles 59-72%,
      newspapers 36-72%) and notes that inflation is typical [TC97 p.628]. TC97 also cites a meta-analytic finding that
      attitudes correlate more strongly with observed behavior than with self-reported behavior (.43 vs. .33) [TC97
      p.632]. Personas answering surveys over-report modestly relative to their enacted behavior (RS.P4).
L4.R9 [SOFT] (E/I) Willingness to pay is framing-sensitive and only weakly attitudinal (E). Contribution framing tracks
      AC more than tax framing does (E, secondary). That tax framing also triggers legitimacy objections (L2.P6) is (I).
L4.R10 [SOFT] (E) Individualism lowers sacrifice and citizenship. Fatalism and egalitarianism lower citizenship
      [VBN99 Table 2].
L4.R11 [SOFT] (E) Correspondence. NEP and PN predict a persona's OVERALL behavior pattern, not each act. Individual acts
      may run against attitude. Do not make every specific behavior attitude-consistent [TC97 pp.620-621].
L4.R12 [SOFT] (E) ABC (attitude x context). The attitude-behavior link is strongest when context is neutral, and
      approaches zero when context strongly compels or prohibits the behavior (an inverted U) [S00 p.415]. Use L4.P7 to
      scale coupling for each behavior.
L4.R13 [SOFT] (E) The more costly or difficult a behavior, the less it depends on attitudes; capabilities and context
      dominate. Attitudinal share of explained variance falls from 59% (thermostat settings) to 25% (major
      investments) [S00 p.416].
L4.R14 [SOFT] (E) There are four causal types: attitudinal, contextual, personal capabilities, and habit. Wherever
      attitude and behavior diverge in the inventory (L4.P6), name a non-attitudinal cause [S00 pp.416-417].

5.5 Stability
Enacted behavior is STATIC within a session. Behavioral intentions are DYNAMIC (Section 9). In longitudinal
simulations, a change in cost or availability can change behavior with no change in L1-L3. Persuasion that raises PN
translates into behavior only as far as constraints and coupling allow.

5.6 Persona System Hook (L4)
- Supplies the persona's concrete behavior inventory (L4.P6): what it actually does in a typical week or year, and the
  practical reasons it doesn't do more.
- Supplies the persona's pattern of support vs. sacrifice ("would you...?" answers).
- Expressed as specific, everyday activities and constraints, never as propensity scores.

6. CX: CONTEXTUAL INPUTS (SOCIAL POSITION, DEMOGRAPHICS, ERA)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

Not a psychological layer. Social position acts twice: it shapes early socialization (L1, L2), and it supplies
opportunities and constraints at L4 [SDG95 p.726] (E).

6.1 Era setting
CX.P0  Calibration era: CORPUS (U.S., ~1990s), PRESENT-DAY U.S., or OTHER (specify). The default is PRESENT-DAY (v2.2).
       - PRESENT-DAY: political identity (L2.P9) is standard; its weights come from survey data (Section 13).
       - Anachronism control: CORPUS-era personas do not reference post-1990s events, technology, or vocabulary.
CX.P1  Ideological group (v2.2): the survey-derived group the persona represents (Section 13). Group names are
       ideological, never party names.
CX.P2  Science literacy/numeracy (5 bands). Moderates worldview effects (L2.R11) [K12 p.733].

6.2 Principle: demographics MODULATE, never DETERMINE
Sociodemographic and political factors usually explain less than 10% of the variance in environmental attitudes or
behavior [TC97 p.622] (E). Generate psychology mainly from the layer above. Demographics only nudge means and coupling.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. RS: SURVEY RESPONSE STYLE (ORTHOGONAL TO SUBSTANCE)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

How the persona REPORTS its psychology on surveys. This is kept separate so that substance and style can be calibrated
independently.

RS.P1  Acquiescence (L / M / H): a tendency to agree regardless of wording. Wording direction can create artificial
       factors, which is why the revised NEP balances pro- and anti-NEP items [DVMJ00 pp.431-432] (E). Higher in
       low-coherence, low-education personas (I). A high-acquiescence persona may agree with both a pro-NEP and an
       anti-NEP version of the same idea.
RS.P2  Midpoint/"Unsure" use (L / M / H). More than 20% chose "Unsure" on items 1, 4, and 14 [DVMJ00 p.434] (E). Higher
       for low-salience, emergent, or low-coherence personas, and on the Limits and Exemptionalism items (I).
RS.P3  Extremity (L / M / H): use of scale endpoints vs. the middle. Absorbs the personal mean rating level that S92
       recommends partialing out [S92 p.56] (E/I).
RS.P4  Social desirability (L / M / H). Raises reported pro-environmental behavior (L4.R8) and softens socially
       disapproved views. Stronger when interviewer-administered (I).
RS.P5  Satisficing/nonresponse (L / M / H): don't-know answers, skipped items, straight-lining on long grids (I).
RS.P6  Open-ended style: length, register, hedging, and use of personal anecdote vs. abstract argument. Tied to education
       and salience (I).

Rule RS.R1 [POP] (E/I): across the persona set, reproduce realistic "Unsure" shares (about 20-25% on items 1, 4, and 14)
and some agreement with both directions of reverse-worded item pairs.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. GENERATIVE PROCEDURE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

Generate top-down. Each step draws mainly from the step above, with noise. All weights below are (I): approximate
standardized values chosen to reproduce the corpus R² targets in Section 10.2. Unstandardized b values from the papers
are NOT usable as weights, because the scales differ. Recalibrate by simulating a population and checking the realized
correlations against Section 10.
v2.2: wherever survey data measure a link (Section 13), group-specific estimates REPLACE these weights. The corpus fixes
the signs and the causal order. Draw L2.P5 and L2.P9 jointly from group data, and add political identity to the L2/L3
equations with data-estimated weights.

Step 0  SET ERA (CX.P0) AND IDEOLOGICAL GROUP (CX.P1). Choose the number of personas per group (e.g., 10-30) so they
        span within-group variation (Section 13).
Step 1  DRAW CX JOINTLY from the group's survey-derived joint distribution (Section 13), not field by field. Include
        the constraint profile.
Step 2  DRAW L1.
        - Choose the peak/trough region and differentiation, nudged by CX (Section 6.3).
        - Derive the SVS scores and the orientation bands (BIO-ALT, EGO, TRAD, OPEN).
        - Check L1.R1-R6.
Step 3  DRAW L2. Code each parent band as -2..+2. Weights:
          NEP     = .45*BIO-ALT - .15*EGO - .20*TRAD + noise(SD ≈ .85)                   target R² ≈ .25-.35
          then CX nudges: liberal +, primary-industry -, young +, educated + (each small)
        - Convert scores to bands using population quantiles that follow L2.R3.
        - Draw the facet profile around the overall NEP using the L2.R4 texture. Spread it by coherence (L2.P3).
        - Draw Cultural Theory axes (L2.P5), weakly linked to L1: EGO and TRAD pull toward Individualist and
          Hierarchical; BIO-ALT pulls toward Egalitarian (I).
        - Derive L2.P6-P8.
Step 4  DRAW L3.
          AC (each object) = .55*NEP + .20*BIO-ALT - .10*TRAD + noise(SD ≈ .75)          target R² ≈ .45
          object weighting: AC-bio is pulled down by EGO (-.15); AC-ego gets no EGO boost
          AR-self, AR-industry, AR-government = .4*AC + Cultural Theory adjustments + noise(SD ≈ .9)
          PN = .40*NEP + .35*AC + .08*BIO-ALT + noise(SD ≈ .70)                          target R² ≈ .55
          then gate PN: if AC ≤ L or (AR-self ≤ L and AR-government ≤ L and AR-industry ≤ L), cap PN at L.
        - Draw efficacy, salience, crystallization, and identity. Identity at Identified or above requires PN ≥ H.
Step 5  DRAW L4.
          Consumer     = .40*PN + .12*BIO-ALT + .10*sacredness - constraint penalty + noise(SD ≈ .90)
          Policy appr. = .45*NEP + .25*PN + noise(SD ≈ .80)
          Sacrifice    = .45*PN + .15*NEP - .10*TRAD - .15*Individualism + noise(SD ≈ .83)
          Citizenship  = .25*PN + .20*AC + .10*NEP + .10*BIO-ALT - .15*EGO - .15*TRAD
                         - .10*Individualism + .15*resources + noise(SD ≈ .84)
          Activism     = rare event: base probability ~.07, raised by identity, BIO-ALT, and OPEN, lowered by TRAD
        - Set coupling (L4.R6). Under Loose coupling, inflate the noise.
        - Write the behavior inventory (L4.P6) from the bands, the constraints, and the base rates in 5.3.
Step 6  DRAW RS from CX and coherence/salience.
Step 7  VALIDATE.
        - Run the per-persona HARD checks (Section 10.6, X-list). On a violation, redraw the offending layer.
        - Identify the persona's realistic tensions (Section 10.5) FROM its residuals. They are an output of the
          draw, not a quota.
Step 8  POPULATION CHECK. Run the [POP] quotas and realized correlations (Section 10). Adjust and redraw if out of
        range.

9. MESSAGE-RESPONSE PROTOCOL (UPDATING WITHIN A CONVERSATION)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

For each environmental message, argument, or survey vignette the persona receives:
(a) VALUE MATCH (L1). Which values does the message invoke? Do they match the persona's top or bottom values? A trade-off
    message resolves by L1.R9.
(b) PLAUSIBILITY AND SOURCE (L2). Is the claim credible given the NEP, the persona's trust in this source (L2.P8),
    the messenger's perceived cultural identity, and how congenial the implied solution is (4.6, L2.R10)?
    Incongruent claims from distrusted sources are discounted or counter-argued (L3.R3).
(c) APPRAISAL UPDATE (L3). Update AC and/or AR.
    - Limit: at most ONE band per message, and only if the message is both value-congruent and credible.
    - Emergent issues (L3.P8) may move up to two bands on first exposure, because the attitude is being formed, not
      changed.
    - Incongruent or low-credibility messages: no change, or a hardening (move away) of one band if the message is
      forceful or moralizing (I).
(d) NORM RECOMPUTE. Recompute PN per Step 4, including the gate. Apply neutralization if the implied action is costly
    (L3.R8).
(e) INTENTION. State a behavioral intention, gated by constraints, coupling, and the channel (L4). Stated intentions
    can change; the enacted behavior inventory does not change within a session.
(f) PERSIST. Carry the updated L3 state forward to later turns. L1 never moves. L2 moves at most one band per session,
    and only through vivid lived experience. RS stays constant.
All step limits are (I). They are conservative defaults meant to counter LLM over-persuadability.

10. CALIBRATION: NOISE, TENSIONS, QUOTAS, AND VALIDATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

10.1 The over-coherence problem
The most common failure of simulated personas is excessive consistency: values perfectly predict worldview, which
predicts norms, which predict behavior. Real data show large unexplained variance at every link. A persona set with
deterministic links is miscalibrated.

10.2 Target unexplained variance (1994 U.S. data [VBN99 p.91]; NEP also SDG95) (E)
    NEP from values: ~63-74%   AC: ~52%   PN: ~44%
    Consumer: ~81%   Sacrifice: ~65%   Citizenship: ~70%   Demonstrating: ~96%

10.3 Target realized correlations across the persona set (E)
    NEP-policy approval ≈ .55   NEP-behavior index ≈ .30-.45   NEP-WTP ≈ .20-.27
    PN-consumer ≈ .41   PN-sacrifice ≈ .55   PN-citizenship ≈ .43
    Behavior channels intercorrelated ≈ .33-.39
    Demographics jointly explain <10% of attitude variance

10.4 Population quotas [POP].
v2.2: population prevalence is NOT a goal; personas represent ideological groups.
- These quotas now serve only as sanity checks when a whole-population set is generated.
- For group-based generation, replace them with WITHIN-GROUP distributions from Section 13.
- POP8 (counter-stereotype) and POP11 (equal depth) still apply within each group.
All shares are (I) unless marked (E).
    POP1  NEP bands per L2.R3: VL 5 / L 15 / M 30 / H 35 / VH 15 %.
    POP2  BIO-ALT bands roughly quintile, with the female mean higher (E: direction).
    POP3  PN: VL 10 / L 20 / M 35 / H 25 / VH 10 %.
    POP4  Identity: None ~50%, Sympathizer ~35%, Identified ~10%, Core ≤5%.
    POP5  Activism at M or above: about 5-10% (E: 7% demonstrated, VBN99 p.88).
    POP6  Salience (L3.P7): VL-L ~50%, M ~30%, H-VH ~20%. For most personas, the environment is not a top concern.
    POP7  AC-bio deniers (VL) present: ≥5%, with reasoned denial (L3.R3).
    POP8  Counter-stereotype quota: ≥15% of personas whose psychology is off-diagonal from their demographic default.
          Examples: a rural conservative man with high NEP and strong local concern; an urban liberal woman with low
          salience and an individualist worldview.
    POP9  Movement critics: some personas with NEP at M or above and movement stance at L or below (L2.R5).
    POP10 Behavior base rates within plausible distance of TC97 Table 1, after report inflation.
    POP11 Equal depth: low-environmental personas are written with the same specificity, intelligence, and internal
          logic as high-environmental ones.

10.5 Realistic tensions (identify from residuals; most personas will show at least one)
    T1  Strong norm, low green consumption: cost, availability, household constraints.
    T2  Strong norm, low citizenship: low income or access, no civic habit.
    T3  Sympathizer who never demonstrates. This is the modal environmentally concerned person.
    T4  "Government should fix it" without blaming government.
    T5  Supports environmental protection; distrusts the environmental movement.
    T6  Accepts the harm is real; rejects regulation as illegitimate (individualist and/or hierarchical worldview).
    T7  Health- or family-driven concern (AC-ego H) with low ecological concern (AC-bio L).
    T8  Low-to-moderate NEP, but strong local issue concern after lived experience.
    T9  Over-reports recycling and other socially desirable acts.
    T10 Stated willingness to pay changes with framing (fund vs. tax).
    T11 Agrees "humans are abusing the environment" while trusting human ingenuity to fix it.
    T12 Benevolence-driven community caretaker who conserves locally, without universalist environmentalism.
    T13 Feels responsible but sees no point ("nothing I do matters"): AR-self H, efficacy L.
    T14 Policy approval H, personal sacrifice L.
    T15 Neutralizes when asked to pay or give something up (L3.R8).

10.6 Implausible patterns: per-persona HARD checks (reject or justify in the spec)
    X1  "Values everything" profile with no trade-offs (L1.R1).
    X2  Benevolence treated as automatically implying environmentalism (L1.R5).
    X3  High EGO + high TRAD + low BIO-ALT with VH NEP and no recorded reason (L2.R1).
    X4  PN at H or above, or AR at H or above, with AC at VL (L3.R1).
    X5  PN at H or above with VL in every behavior channel (L4.R1).
    X6  All five behavior channels in the same band (L4.R2).
    X7  Willingness to sacrifice at H or above combined with AC at VL. Follows from L3.R1 plus the PN/NEP dependence of
        sacrifice [VBN99 Table 1].
    X8  Denial without reasons, or a caricatured denier (L3.R3 HARD part).
    X9  Psychology set purely from demographics (6.2).
    X10 CORPUS-era persona with anachronistic knowledge (CX.P0).
    Rarity checks (SOFT; flag if the population exceeds them): opposing value poles both top-ranked (L1.R3, ≤5%);
    activism without identity (L4.R3).

13. DATA CALIBRATION: IDEOLOGICAL GROUPS FROM CURRENT SURVEY DATA (v2.2; finalized after analysis)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

13.1 Principle
Theory (Sections 1-10) fixes the structure. Survey data define the ideological groups, their layer profiles, their
within-group variation, and effect magnitudes. Group names describe ideology and values, never party.

13.2 Data sources (U.S. only; folder "Survey Data")
- WVS-US 2017 (N = 2,596; probability web panel; weight W_WEIGHT; effective n ≈ 1,580). Rich values and worldview
  proxies. Environmental content limited to Q111 (environment vs. growth), Q79, and Q99.
- Pew ATP W158, Oct 2024 (N = 9,593; probability panel; WEIGHT_W158). Climate beliefs, policy support, responsibility,
  emotions, confidence in actors and scientists; party, lean, strength, ideology, religion, attendance. No values items.
- VCSI, Jul 2026 (N = 2,528; opt-in online; light weights). Consumer sustainability and climate concern (VCSI index);
  party, ideology, vote. No values or religion items. A codebook is needed for its unlabeled items.

13.3 Layer mapping (proxies; (I) until validated)
- L1 values: WVS importance items (Q1-Q6), child qualities (autonomy vs. obedience), religiosity (Q164, Q171, Q173),
  moral justifiability (Q177-Q195), materialism/postmaterialism (Y002), Welzel indices.
- L2.P5 GRID: equality vs. incentives (Q106), gender roles (Q28-Q35), respect for authority (Q45), Welzel equality and
  authority components.
- L2.P5 GROUP: private vs. government ownership (Q107), government vs. individual responsibility (Q108), competition
  (Q109).
- L2.P8 trust: WVS confidence items (Q64-Q89); ATP CONF_a-j, SCM3/SCM5.
- L2.P9 political identity: WVS Q223 (vote intention) and Q240; ATP party, lean, strength, and F_IDEO; VCSI party, lean,
  ideology, and vote.
- L2.P1 / L3: ATP EN7/EN8 (causes), CLIM9 (local impact), CLIMENO (actors doing enough ~ AR), CLIMFEEL2 (affect);
  VCSI climate worry, harm, and responsibility items.
- L4: ATP CCPOLICY (policy approval), CCFUTRYOU2 (sacrifice); VCSI consumer commitment, willingness to pay, sacrifice.

13.4 Pipeline
1. SEGMENT in WVS on L1/L2 indicators only (latent profile/class or clustering, survey-weighted). Party and ideology
   DESCRIBE the groups; they do not define them. Target 5-10 groups; the effective n favors 5-7. Choose k by fit,
   stability (bootstrap), interpretability, and minimum group size.
2. BRIDGE MODEL. Within WVS, predict group membership from the variables shared with the other surveys: party,
   ideology, religious attendance, age, sex, race/ethnicity, education, region, marital status, and (WVS-ATP only)
   comparable confidence items. Report accuracy. Groups that the bridges cannot separate are flagged.
3. SOFT ASSIGNMENT. Apply the bridge model to ATP and VCSI respondents as membership probabilities, not hard labels.
   Use multiple imputation to carry uncertainty forward.
4. GROUP ENVIRONMENTAL PROFILES. Compute probability-weighted (and survey-weighted) environmental distributions per
   group in ATP (beliefs, policy) and VCSI (consumer, concern).
5. THEORY FILL. Where the data cannot distinguish groups (flagged in step 2), differentiate their L2-L4 profiles by
   applying BL theory to their WVS value and worldview profiles. Tag the result (I).
6. VALIDATION. Check WVS Q111 against ATP CLIM5/CCPROP, and WVS Q79 against ATP CLIMENO_d, for plausibility. Do a
   time-gap sensitivity check (2017 vs. 2024 party x ideology x religiosity joint distribution). Compare with any
   external segmentation.
7. PERSONA PARAMETERS. Group means and within-group variance per parameter feed Section 8 draws.

13.5 Data cautions
- WVS: negative missing codes; Q289 overcounts "no religion" (use Q164, Q171, Q173); Q33-Q35 use a 3-point US format;
  IDs and dates were lost in the CSV.
- ATP: split-form blanks; DK/refused were converted to blanks; stray pasted columns must be dropped.
- VCSI: opt-in sample; codebook needed; contains date of birth (exclude as personal data).
- Period differences (2017/2024/2026) confound group comparisons across surveys.
